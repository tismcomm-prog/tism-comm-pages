The two mechanisms

05, sections 5.2 and 5.3

The Double Duty Hand

Cutting by hand makes one hand do two competing jobs: generate the force, and steer the line. Both run through the same small muscles and the same thumb joint.

It explains the pain

The load concentrates in thumb and wrist and scales with the size of the pile, not the difficulty of any one cut. Hence sore, numb, cramped.

It explains the failure on thick board

Thicker board raises the force needed, and there is no adjustment available, because the only source of force is the hand.

It explains the cuts

Fatigue in job one degrades job two. The slip happens on box nine, not box one.

The Lift Guide

The fixed metal fin behind the wheel slides under the material and lifts it into a blade that is already spinning under its own power. Your hand is left with one job: push forward.

Proof, unprompted

"The blade and guide lifted the carpet from the floor and allowed me to get as straight a cut as I possibly could."

Why it is ours to take

Competitors all have a guide, but their positioning is built on the wheel: RPM, torque, blade hardness. To claim the lift they would have to demote the motor they spent the page selling.

The hero shot it implies

Macro, side angle. The fin under the liner, the strip separating behind. The operator's hand relaxed and open, thumb straight. The argument is in the hand, not the blade.

Objections nobody handles

04, the open goals

It will not cut the boxes I need it for

Thick, double wall, stapled seam. Competitors answer with a bigger spec, which does not work on a buyer already let down by a spec. Two reviewers say the fix is holding it at an angle.

The safety lock will be what hurts me

Real evidence in the corpus that this happens. No competitor page shows how the lock behaves in the hand.

It is a two hand job and I am alone

"i couldn't cut anything bigger than a bread box alone." Unaddressed everywhere.

The battery dies and it is trash

Answer with the replacement policy. The corpus already holds the proof: a snapped blade bolt replaced free, and the rating went up because of it.

I own a working tool and I am faster

Marcus. Concede the speed on small boxes, then take the large box, the blade changes and the cuts.

It costs more than it looks like it should

Resolved in the reviews by regret at waiting, not by a value argument. "The price of this was a little high, but I wish I caved sooner."

Hold the page against this

Derived from the brain
  • Does one named avatar own the page? A page written to all five is written to nobody.
  • Does it open where that avatar's awareness stage starts? Dana on the outcome, Ruth on the pain, Marcus by agreeing with him.
  • Is the mechanism named before the promise? The Double Duty Hand before any claim about ease.
  • Does it claim reduction rather than removal? Effortlessness contradicts our own four star reviews.
  • Does it state the thickness limit before the buyer finds it? The buyers who knew left four and five stars.
  • Does it show the safety lock? Mandatory on anything aimed at Ruth.
  • Does it concede the utility knife on small boxes? Without that concession Marcus is gone by line two.
  • Does it avoid every item on the sameness list? No RPM, no wattage, no blade metallurgy, no "cuts like butter".
  • Are the quotes verbatim, typos intact? Tidied quotes read as written by us, because they were.
  • Does it answer at least three of the six open objections? They are open on every competitor page.
  • Is the proof volume based rather than cut based? A bin that closes beats another blade going through card.
  • Does anything on it claim curves, or garment fabric? The source contradicts both.
